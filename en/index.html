03 / FROM RULES TO PRACTICE

GDPR, explained
in plain language.

First identify the data and the purpose. Then clarify the legal basis, access and responsibilities with the Data Protection Officer (DPO).

§

Publicly available does not mean free for every use.

Posts, comments, author profiles and activity histories may identify a person. A research purpose does not remove the need for assessment.

Explore research data ↗
01 When is information personal?

When it relates to an identified or identifiable person. Combining otherwise general details can make someone identifiable.

Training example: a postcode combined with a specific senior job title may point to a single person.

02 What is a valid legal basis?

Consent, contract, legal obligation, vital interests, public task or legitimate interests — depending on the processing. Research does not automatically qualify as a public task; the DPO checks the legal foundation and conditions.

Special categories also require an applicable Article 9 condition. Consent is one possible basis, not a universal solution.

03 Pseudonymised = anonymous?

Replacing names with codes does not guarantee anonymity. Assess the actual possibility of re-identification.

In C‑413/23 P, the Court clarifies that the assessment of pseudonymised data also depends on the recipient and the means available to them. Do not call a dataset anonymous simply because it has no names.

Court of Justice of the EU · C‑413/23 P ↗
04 How should an access request be handled?

Forward it to the DPO immediately. Identity and information about other people are checked before responding. The usual deadline is one month; Article 12(3) allows a further two months under its conditions, with notice within the first month.

In the scenario: an applicant requests registrations, newsletter records, their CV and interview notes. Do not ignore the request or send an unchecked full archive.

05 Can I share with an important partner?

Partnership and urgency are not sufficient grounds. Clarify the recipient, purpose, permissibility, minimum scope, terms and secure channel with the responsible team.

In the scenario: conference contacts are requested for another initiative. Hold the transfer until the DPO has reviewed it.

Legal clarifications: GDPR — Articles 4–6, 9, 12, 15 and 33 ↗. A learning guide; specific decisions should be coordinated with the DPO.

Better models.

Before collecting, scraping or using data to train an AI model, check provenance, rights and restrictions. Public APIs have terms too.

PROVENANCE → PURPOSE → PERMISSION
01

Check the source and rights

Review the licence, contract, intended purpose and reuse restrictions. When scraping, check robots.txt, stated AI/TDM restrictions and TDMRep; technical access alone does not grant permission.

02

Create a dataset card

Document provenance, version, licence, personal data, transformations and limitations. The training refers to Dataset Cards and the Croissant format.

03

Track risks and the lifecycle

Check representativeness and bias; control versions and access. Discuss new processing with the DPO for the record of processing activities and any need for an impact assessment.

    06 / WORKING WITH THE DPO

    When to engage with the DPO?

    Contact the DPO ↗
    Inform immediately Obtain approval Consult before acting

    Inform the DPO Immediately

    A breach or a suspected one

    Lost laptop, wrong recipient, phishing click, data on display. Inform the DPO and keep the evidence.

    Someone exercising their rights

    “What do you hold about me?”, “Delete my data”. Forward it immediately to the DPO. GATE has one month to respond.

    Data in the wrong place

    Personal data on a personal device, personal cloud or an unapproved system.

    A dataset nobody owns

    Data or a dataset with no owner, no purpose or no retention rule.

    The DPO Must Approve

    Access to or sharing of Restricted data

    This needs explicit DPO and legal counsel approval.

    Exceptions to the retention schedule

    Keeping data longer, or deleting it earlier, than the schedule says.

    Deleting data at the end of retention

    The data owner informs the DPO, and the DPO confirms there is no legal hold before anything is deleted.

    Legal holds

    Only the DPO can issue one.

    Consult the DPO Before Acting

    A new purpose

    Using existing data for something it wasn’t collected for, such as adding event attendees to the newsletter.

    A new tool or system, especially AI

    A new AI tool, an analytics platform, a large data extract, or any major system change. A DPIA may be needed.

    Automated decisions or profiling

    For example, AI that screens CVs or scores people. A DPIA is required beforehand.

    Sharing data outside GATE

    Partners, suppliers, students, hackathons, other institutes. A contract or data-sharing agreement may be needed.

    Reasons for data processing

    Designing a consent form, or collecting explicit consent for health or other sensitive data.

    Writing a privacy notice

    For a new form, event, study or recruitment campaign.

    Direct marketing

    Newsletters, campaigns, mailing lists.

    Any special category data

    Health, biometrics, ethnicity and similar, including inferred data.

    Unsure about the legal basis, retention period or security measures

    These GATE procedures follow the three training slides “When to contact the DPO”. DPIA means Data Protection Impact Assessment.
